Fit the Mess

Real points do not line up politely. Least squares finds the line whose misses are smallest overall.

The idea

A residual is actual y − predicted y. Positive means the point sits above the line; negative means it sits below.

The best-fit line uses every point. Its slope is the shared x-and-y movement divided by the x movement squared. Then the line is placed through the mean point.

slope = Σ((x − mean x)(y − mean y)) ÷ Σ((x − mean x)²)

intercept = mean y − slope · mean x

residual = actual y − (slope · x + intercept)

The first plot shows the data and fitted line. The second puts every residual around zero, so the misses are visible instead of hidden inside one score.

Challenges

  1. Change the middle point from [3, 5] to [3, 4]. The points become more line-like. Watch the largest residual shrink.
  2. Change the last point to [5, 10]. One far point can pull the fitted line; the residual plot shows which misses that creates elsewhere.
  3. Add [6, 7] to the data array. Nothing else needs changing — every sum and both plots use the array's length.
  4. Change every y value by adding 2. Predict which of slope and intercept will change before you run it.
  5. Replace actualY - fit(x) with fit(x) - actualY. The residual picture flips, but the squared error stays the same because squaring removes the sign.
